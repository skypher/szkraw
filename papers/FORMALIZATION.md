# Formalization notes for the three papers

The Lean development (`lean/`) was written against the full manuscript `paper.tex`, whose numbering differs from the three papers in this directory. This file keeps the material that the papers only point to: the concordance between the two numberings, the full table of Lean declarations, and the notes on how each Lean statement matches the text. Build and audit: `make lean`, `make axioms` (only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` are allowed).

## Statement-by-statement notes (numbering of paper.tex; LaTeX source)

Theorem, lemma and section numbers below refer to `paper.tex`; use the concordance above for the papers.

The entry for Theorem~2.1 includes a positive finite orthogonality measure, distinct support points, the terminal relation, and all finite product identities. The entry for Theorem~5.3 includes the explicit radius, positive triangle margin, and exact zeros outside the triangle. In Sections~3 and~4 the algebra $A$ is the iterated quadratic extension \path{Szkraw.tower}, whose generator adjoined at stage $N+1-j$ is $z_j$ with the square relation \eqref{eq:algebra}; subsets $S$ are encoded as $\{0,1\}$-vectors of length $N$, $\tau$ is \path{Szkraw.towerTrace}, $E_k$ is \path{Szkraw.elementary} and $t_k$ is \path{Szkraw.normalizer}. Lemma~2.2 is stated with $p=1/r\geq1$ in both directions. Lemma~3.3 is stated for $k\geq1$; for $k=0$ it reads $E_1=E_1$, where $E_1$ is the sum of the generators (\path{Szkraw.elementary_one}). In Proposition~3.4 Lean states that every product $E_mE_n$ with $m,n\leq N$ is a combination of $E_0,\ldots,E_N$ with nonnegative coefficients; that $B$ is generated by $E_1$ and the independence of the $E_k$ are \path{Szkraw.positive_subalgebra_full}. In Lemma~4.1 the character is an $\mathbb R$-algebra homomorphism with $z_j\mapsto pq^{N-j}$. In Corollary~5.4 Lean states, for the explicit arrays, \eqref{eq:basic}, the failure of the constant-ratio hypothesis, nonnegative linearization and the margin of Theorem~5.3; for its last sentence it gives, for every $0<r<1$, an open set in the subspace of arrays satisfying the equalities in \eqref{eq:basic} that contains the Krawtchouk array at $r$ and consists of arrays with nonnegative linearization. In Corollary~6.1 Lean states nonnegative linearization of the array \eqref{eq:parameters} with $q=1$ and $r=p_0/(1-p_0)$, the orthogonality of its polynomials for the binomial weights at the nodes $1-j/(N(1-p_0))$, and their value $1$ at $j=0$; the sharpness remark in its proof is \path{Szkraw.krawtchouk_range_sharp}, stated for $N\geq1$ and $1/2<p_0<1$ with the value $g(1,1;1)=(1-r)/N$. In Corollary~6.2, ``outside the Krawtchouk recurrence class'' is formalized as disagreement of $b_i+c_i$ with the profile of every Krawtchouk array. Proposition~6.3 is formalized only through formulas transcribed by hand from Koelink: \path{Szkraw.koelink_relation_32} (his relation (3.2) for the generators of the tower) and \path{Szkraw.koelink_recurrence_71} (the coefficients of his Theorem~7.1 divided by $p(1-q^N)$ are those of \eqref{eq:parameters}, and for $q>1$ every solution of his recurrence with $\phi(0)=1$ is $R_d(x_f)$); Lean checks agreement with the transcription, not its faithfulness, and his Hecke-algebra construction and the dual $q$-Krawtchouk interpretation are not formalized. The entries for Section~7 state nonnegativity of the coefficients $g(m,n;k)$: for Theorem~7.10 and Corollary~7.11 on all triples with two indices of sum at most $N$, and for Theorems~7.12, 7.15 and~7.16 and Corollaries~7.13 and~7.17 on all triples. Lemma~7.14 and the identities of Theorem~7.15 are stated for the matrices $R_m(J)$ and $R'_t(J)=\Pi R'_t(\Pi J\Pi)\Pi$. Theorem~7.1 is proved in Lean through Corollary~7.9 with $q=1$, and Lemma~7.4 through the decomposition, whose components are Krawtchouk arrays; the symmetry of its measure holds for every finite realization of an array satisfying \eqref{eq:basic} with $b\equiv0$ (\path{Szkraw.realization_symmetric}). In Corollary~7.7, ``not of the form \eqref{eq:parameters}'' is formalized as the failure of the hypotheses of Theorem~2.1 and as disagreement with every array \eqref{eq:parameters} in the Lean parametrization; its last sentence follows from the explicit coefficient box of positive radius in \path{Szkraw.symmetric_criterion}; the open set with $2N-1$ free coefficients of its last sentence is \path{Szkraw.symmetric_open}, an open ball of free coefficients around those of \eqref{eq:qarray} on which the hypothesis of Theorem~7.6 holds. For Corollary~7.9, when $N\geq2$, Lean proves that both normalized components satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~2.1 and are the arrays \eqref{eq:parameters} with parameters $(N,q,r)$ and $(N-1,q,r/q)$ (\path{Szkraw.ps_even_params}, \path{Szkraw.ps_odd_params}); \path{Szkraw.parity_split} also covers $N=1$, where the odd component is the constant polynomial alone. In Theorem~7.8 the identity for $g(2a,2b+1;2c+1)$ is stated with the unnormalized odd component, which has the same product coefficients (\path{Szkraw.product_normalize}). The finite realization holds for every array satisfying \eqref{eq:basic} (\path{Szkraw.finite_realization}). The proofs formalize the cone argument and the identity $g(m,n;k)\int R_k^2\,d\mu=g(m,k;n)\int R_n^2\,d\mu$ in matrix form. The entry for Theorem~8.1 includes the same finite realization as the first and the nonnegativity of every coefficient $g(m,n;k)$; its proof formalizes Lemmas~8.3 and~8.4, the operator induction, and Lemma~8.2 in converse form: the solution of~\eqref{eq:kernel-rec} yields the linearization coefficients. The remark after the proof of Theorem~8.1 is formalized as identities for all real $u$ and $v$ (two-parameter form, \path{Szkraw.lc_twoParam}) and for all real $r$ (Bernstein form, \path{Szkraw.lc_bernstein}, whose coefficients $B_s$ are $\binom Ds$ times the Bernstein coefficients); \path{Szkraw.constantRatio_twoParam} identifies the arrays of Theorem~8.1 with this family, and the proof formalizes the operator induction coefficientwise in $t$. All of them have compiled proofs without added axioms or admitted goals; the axiom audit reports only \path{propext}, \path{Classical.choice} and \path{Quot.sound}. Lemma~7.2 is stated for every commutative real algebra with elements $y_1,\ldots,y_N$ satisfying $y_j^2=(1-r)y_j+r$ whose squarefree monomials form a basis, which determines $A$ up to isomorphism, and for $r\neq-1$; \path{Szkraw.cube_model} shows that the real functions on $\{1,-r\}^N$ form such an algebra. Its basis assertion is stated as linear independence of the $f_ly_S$ together with equality of their span and the ideal $f_lA$. Lemma~7.3 is stated for $N\geq1$, $p_0<1$ and $r=p_0/(1-p_0)$, in every commutative real algebra satisfying these relations, without a basis hypothesis; its recurrence is an equality in $A\times A^N$, which restricts to $V$, and \path{Szkraw.Uvec_top} states $U_{2N+1}=0$ separately. Lemma~9.2 is stated for $N\geq2$ as the derivative at $\varepsilon=0$ of $g(N,N;N)$ along $K_{N,1}+\varepsilon v$. Its Lean proof replaces the beta integrals by an explicit matrix: the derivative $X$ of $R_N^\varepsilon(J_\varepsilon)$ satisfies $[J,X]=[\Pi,E]$ and $e_0^\mathsf TX=0$, and $Z_{mn}=\binom Nn(-1)^{n+s}/(2\binom{N-1}s)$ for $m+n=2s+1$, $Z_{mn}=0$ for $m+n$ even, satisfies $ZJ-JZ=e_Ne_N^\mathsf T$ on the columns $n\geq1$; hence the derivative equals $\operatorname{tr}((Z\Pi-\Pi Z)E)$, which is evaluated entry by entry. In Theorem~9.4 the set of arrays is a family of sets indexed by $N$ whose members satisfy $g(m,n;k)\geq0$, admission is Definition~9.3, and the second statement is the vanishing of the derivative for every $N\geq1$ above the degrees. Lemma~9.5 is stated with $U(x)=\int_0^1\log|x-t|\,dt$ (\path{Szkraw.Ulog}) for $f$ complex differentiable on an open set containing $\Omega$, as a bound $C\sqrt N\,e^{-\eta N}$ for every $N\geq1$; the cycle of its proof is the boundary of a finite union of squares of an axis-parallel grid with mesh $1/M$ for a positive integer $M$ and origin $\sqrt2(1+i)$, so that no node $k/N$ lies on a grid line (\path{Szkraw.residue_identity}), and the Riemann-sum and Stirling estimates are \path{Szkraw.riemann_lower} and \path{Szkraw.factorial_le_stirling}. In Corollary~9.6 and Proposition~9.9~(2), a rule on $[0,1]$ is analytic on an open set containing $\Omega$ if it is the restriction of a function complex differentiable there (\path{Szkraw.AnalyticOnOmega}); the second statement of Corollary~9.6 is the bound $|s_N|\,C\sqrt N\,e^{-\eta N}$ on the derivative for every $N\geq2$, and the example is stated for every $N\geq2$. In Proposition~9.9~(2) the boundary layers vanish beyond a fixed index $K$, the formulas for $\delta b_n$ and $\delta c_n$ are assumed for $1\leq n\leq N-1$ and $1\leq n\leq N$ and all large $N$, and the hypothesis is that of the first statement; The first statement of Corollary~9.6 is obtained by specializing Proposition~9.9~(2) to zero boundary layers. In Proposition~9.10 the direction is given in the coordinates $b_1,\ldots,b_{N-1},c_1,\ldots,c_N$ of Section~9, the hypothesis is that the derivative of $g(N,N;N)$ at $\varepsilon=0$ is negative, and the conclusion is that $K_{N,r}+\varepsilon v$ satisfies \eqref{eq:basic} and has $g(N,N;N)<0$; \path{Szkraw.productMatrix_contDiffAt} gives the smoothness used in its proof. In Corollary~9.11, $\rho_N(r)$ is the supremum of the radii with the stated property, and exact order means $c_1(1-r)^N\leq\rho_N(r)\leq c_2(1-r)^N$ for all $r$ in an interval $(r_0,1)$. Lemma~9.7 is stated for the spectral measure of every array satisfying \eqref{eq:basic}, whose node $x_0=1$ exists by \path{Szkraw.spectral_top_node}; the polynomials of $\mu_\varepsilon$ are identified by orthogonality and their value $1$ at $x_0$ (\path{Szkraw.lower_measure}), and both parts are derivatives at $\varepsilon=0$. In Proposition~9.8, $\Lambda_N$ is an explicit finite sum bounding the absolute ratios of the two first-order rates with nonzero $r$-rate (\path{Szkraw.lamN}), the lowered measure is identified by orthogonality and normalization, and the neighborhood is a coefficient box of some positive radius around the normalized array; the proof uses an explicit formula for the coefficients of $K_{N,r}$ (\path{Szkraw.kraw_g_formula}).
